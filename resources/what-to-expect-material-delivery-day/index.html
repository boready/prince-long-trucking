Delivery Planning

What to Expect on Material Delivery Day

Know what happens during rock, dirt, sand or millings delivery and how to prepare a safe dump location.

The driver confirms the placement area

A clear, firm dump location should be identified before the truck enters the property. The driver may decline an unsafe route or placement request.

Truck size changes access needs

Loaded dump trucks are heavy and require more turning room than passenger vehicles. Soft lawns, narrow culverts and steep entrances may not support the truck.

Unloading requires overhead clearance

The raised dump body can contact power lines, tree limbs or structures. Keep people and vehicles away from the unloading zone.

Weather may change the schedule

Rain, frozen ground, road restrictions or supplier delays can affect delivery. Keep contact information current so scheduling changes can be communicated quickly.

Questions to answer before ordering

Be ready to provide the project address, measurements, desired material, estimated quantity, preferred delivery window and photos of the access route. Mention gates, soft ground, overhead utilities, steep slopes, culverts and limited turnaround areas. These details help prevent delays and allow the hauler to determine whether the requested placement is safe.

Local planning for Northeast Kansas

Soil conditions and weather can change quickly across Wamego, Manhattan, Junction City and nearby communities. Heavy rain may soften access routes, while freeze-thaw cycles can expose drainage and base problems. Build flexibility into the schedule and avoid placing valuable material before the site is ready to receive, spread and compact it.
