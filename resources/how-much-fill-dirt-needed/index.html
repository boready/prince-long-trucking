How Much Fill Dirt Does My Project Need?

Estimate fill dirt volume for grading and low-area projects, including settlement, compaction and site-shape considerations.

Planning note: Material selection and quantities vary by site. Confirm product specifications with your supplier or contractor before ordering.
In this guide

  2. Loose and compacted volume differ
  3. Do not bury drainage problems
  4. Structural fills need professional input

Measure the footprint and average depth

For a rectangular area, multiply length by width by average depth in feet, then divide by 27 for cubic yards. Irregular slopes require several measurements or a contractor's grade calculation.

Loose and compacted volume differ

Delivered dirt is loose. After spreading and compaction, it occupies less volume. The amount of shrinkage depends on soil type, moisture and compaction effort.

Do not bury drainage problems

Filling a low area without giving water a safe path can redirect runoff toward a building or neighboring property. Establish the intended finished grade before ordering.

Structural fills need professional input

Building pads, retaining walls and load-bearing areas may require engineered soil, lift thickness, moisture conditioning and compaction testing. A delivery estimate is not a substitute for design requirements.

Questions to answer before ordering

Be ready to provide the project address, measurements, desired material, estimated quantity, preferred delivery window and photos of the access route. Mention gates, soft ground, overhead utilities, steep slopes, culverts and limited turnaround areas. These details help prevent delays and allow the hauler to determine whether the requested placement is safe.

Local planning for Northeast Kansas

Soil conditions and weather can change quickly across Wamego, Manhattan, Junction City and nearby communities. Heavy rain may soften access routes, while freeze-thaw cycles can expose drainage and base problems. Build flexibility into the schedule and avoid placing valuable material before the site is ready to receive, spread and compact it.
