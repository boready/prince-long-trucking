Asphalt Millings vs. Gravel for Driveways

Compare asphalt millings and gravel for Kansas driveways, including cost, compaction, dust, drainage and maintenance.

Millings can bind under the right conditions

Recycled asphalt contains residual binder that may help particles knit together during warm weather and compaction. Results depend heavily on material quality, placement and equipment.

Gravel is easier to reshape

Crushed aggregate can be graded and replenished over time. It may generate dust or move under traffic, but local availability and predictable gradations make it widely used.

Base preparation still matters

Neither product will perform well over deep mud, standing water or an unstable subgrade. Correct drainage and a strong base are more important than the surface material alone.

Availability can decide the choice

Asphalt millings are a recycled byproduct and may not be consistently available. Request pricing early and be prepared to compare suitable gravel alternatives.

Questions to answer before ordering

Be ready to provide the project address, measurements, desired material, estimated quantity, preferred delivery window and photos of the access route. Mention gates, soft ground, overhead utilities, steep slopes, culverts and limited turnaround areas. These details help prevent delays and allow the hauler to determine whether the requested placement is safe.

Local planning for Northeast Kansas

Soil conditions and weather can change quickly across Wamego, Manhattan, Junction City and nearby communities. Heavy rain may soften access routes, while freeze-thaw cycles can expose drainage and base problems. Build flexibility into the schedule and avoid placing valuable material before the site is ready to receive, spread and compact it.
