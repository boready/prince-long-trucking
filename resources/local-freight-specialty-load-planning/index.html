How to Plan a Local Freight or Specialty Load

Plan local freight and specialty hauling by documenting dimensions, weight, loading method, route and delivery requirements.

Plan local freight and specialty hauling by documenting dimensions, weight, loading method, route and delivery requirements.

Planning note: Material selection and quantities vary by site. Confirm product specifications with your supplier or contractor before ordering.
In this guide
  1. Define the load completely
  2. Explain loading and unloading
  3. Check route restrictions
  4. Use one point of contact

Define the load completely

Provide weight, dimensions, quantity, packaging and photos. Note fragile surfaces, center of gravity and whether weather protection is required.

Explain loading and unloading

Identify forklifts, cranes, docks or ramps available at both locations. Waiting time and special handling should be discussed before dispatch.

Check route restrictions

Oversize dimensions, bridge limits, construction zones and local delivery hours may affect the route or require permits.

Use one point of contact

A reliable contact at pickup and delivery reduces delays. Share phone numbers, site instructions and any appointment requirements with the carrier.

Questions to answer before ordering

Be ready to provide the project address, measurements, desired material, estimated quantity, preferred delivery window and photos of the access route. Mention gates, soft ground, overhead utilities, steep slopes, culverts and limited turnaround areas. These details help prevent delays and allow the hauler to determine whether the requested placement is safe.

Local planning for Northeast Kansas

Soil conditions and weather can change quickly across Wamego, Manhattan, Junction City and nearby communities. Heavy rain may soften access routes, while freeze-thaw cycles can expose drainage and base problems. Build flexibility into the schedule and avoid placing valuable material before the site is ready to receive, spread and compact it.
